# History of Artificial Intelligence

**1940-1974 — Birth and Golden Years**  
The first electronic computers emerge during World War II. Turing lays the theoretical foundations (computation theory, Turing Test), Shannon develops information theory, and McCulloch and Pitts propose the first artificial neuron model in 1943. The name "Artificial Intelligence" is officially coined at the 1956 Dartmouth Workshop, with pioneers like McCarthy, Minsky, Shannon, Newell, and Simon. Early concrete results appear in symbolic reasoning, problem solving (e.g., GPS), and NLP (e.g., Eliza). The mood is one of great enthusiasm, with very optimistic predictions — Minsky predicted in 1970 that human-level AI would arrive within 3-8 years.

**1974-1980 — The First Winter**  
Results fall short of expectations and funding collapses. Main problems: limited computational power, combinatorial explosion, datasets too small. Neural networks are scaled back.

**1980-1987 — New Spring**  
Expert systems emerge (knowledge + logical rules). Neural networks get new life from the Backpropagation algorithm (Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams, 1986). Japan invests heavily in the "Fifth Generation" project for intelligent computers.

**1987-1993 — The Second Winter**  
The Japanese project fails, funding stops again. Specialized hardware loses competitiveness against PCs. Expert systems only deliver results in very specific fields, and neural networks fail to scale to complex problems.

**1993-2011 — Modern Times**  
Hardware keeps getting more powerful. Bayesian Networks, intelligent agents, robust classifiers (SVM, Random Forest, Adaboost), Hidden Markov Models, and mature hand-crafted feature extraction techniques (SIFT, bag of words) emerge. These are the years of Deep Blue (1997, chess), Watson (2011, Jeopardy), and the DARPA Grand Challenges on autonomous driving. Widespread success follows in computer vision, biometrics, speech recognition, robotics, and medical diagnosis.

**2011-today — Deep Learning**  
CNNs (introduced by LeCun in 1989, but limited by lack of data and computing power) explode in 2012 with AlexNet, which wins ImageNet by a wide margin, kicking off the deep learning revolution in computer vision. In 2016, speech recognition and machine translation reach/surpass human performance. Starting in 2018, Large Language Models based on the Transformer architecture arrive: GPT-3 (2020) → ChatGPT (2022) → GPT-4 (2023), with unexpected reasoning capabilities emerging ("sparks of AGI"). From 2023 to 2026, competition broadens to include Google (Gemini), Anthropic (Claude), and other models, including open source ones.

The document closes by connecting this historical arc to broader themes: AGI/ASI, technological singularity, the AI economic boom (NVIDIA, very high U.S. salaries), and the ethical/safety concerns raised by figures like Hawking, Hinton, and Bengio.
